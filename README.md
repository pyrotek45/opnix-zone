# Opnix Zone

A modular, fully procedural shooter toolkit in pure Rust (Bevy 0.16), and the
games built from it. There are no asset files anywhere in this repository:
every model, map, sky and sound is generated by code.

| Game | What it is |
|------|------------|
| **Ember Atoll** (`games/ember_atoll`) | Co-op defense. Mine and automate the islands by day, hold the beacon against robotic sea creatures by night. Also includes a pure **Horde** mode. |
| **Iron Town** (`games/iron_town`) | Team deathmatch in a procedural town of enterable two-storey buildings, 1v1 to 6v6 with bots in every empty slot. Pre-match lobby, create-a-class, a gunsmith with a live 3D preview, scoreboard and rematches. |

Both games are thin: a content catalog, rules, and a small director. Movement,
weapons, AI, bots, networking, rendering, menus and audio are shared crates.

## Run

```bash
cargo run --release -p ember_atoll
```

```bash
cargo run --release -p iron_town
```

Always use `--release` to play; debug builds are much slower. Linux needs the
usual Bevy system packages (ALSA, udev, X11 or Wayland, Vulkan); `shell.nix`
provides them on Nix. Add `--no-default-features` to build without audio.

Shortcuts past the menu: `--solo`, `--host`, `--join ADDRESS`, `--mode N`,
`--seed N`, `--teams N`. **MODEL VIEWER** in the main menu shows every
operator pose side by side.

## Playing online (no cloud server needed)

One player picks **HOST GAME** and plays too. Friends pick **JOIN GAME**.

* **Same network (LAN):** the host shows up under *Games on your network* on
  the Join screen - click it. Nothing to type.
* **Over the internet:** the host's lobby and pause menu show the addresses
  to share. When you host, the game asks your router to open TCP port
  **27650** automatically (UPnP). If the router allows it, the lobby shows
  your internet address to give friends. If not, forward that port by hand,
  or put everyone on a free mesh VPN such as Tailscale or ZeroTier and use the
  host's VPN address. There is no relay server.

What makes it play well online:

* **Lag compensation.** Other players are shown 0.1 s in the past from a
  buffer (smooth even with jitter), and every input tells the host what
  moment you were looking at. When you fire, the host rewinds everyone to
  that moment (up to 0.35 s) before checking the hit: if it was on your
  screen, it hits. In co-op the sea creatures are rewound the same way.
* **Instant shooting.** Your own recoil, muzzle flash, sound, tracer and ammo
  count happen immediately on your machine; the host confirms in the
  background. Rockets and thrown gear leave your hand at once too. Quick taps
  between network sends are never lost, and weapon swaps show at once.
* **Clean failures.** Ping is on the scoreboard (and in the corner with
  *FPS / PING* on). Silent connections time out on both sides, and you are
  told why you were disconnected.
* **Reconnecting.** If your connection drops, the game retries the host on
  its own (and leaves a RECONNECT button if that fails). The host keeps your
  seat for ten minutes: you come back on the same team with your score,
  class and weapons.
* **Players:** name tags over teammates, enemy names under your crosshair,
  text chat (**T**), a kill-cam on your killer while you wait to respawn,
  spawn protection, spawns away from the enemy, duplicate names told apart,
  and everyone sees each other's real attachments.

Headless dedicated servers are included too, for running a match on a spare
machine. The Iron Town one starts each match on its own after a short lobby:

```bash
cargo run --release -p iron_town --bin iron_server -- --teams 4 --score 30
```

```bash
cargo run --release -p ember_atoll --bin ember_server -- --mode 0 --port 27650
```

Press **V** in a match (or launch with `--spectate`) to watch other players
over the shoulder; the camera cuts to whoever is in a firefight.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Gamepad |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| Move / look | WASD / mouse | left / right stick |
| Fire / aim | LMB / RMB | RT / LT |
| Reload / use station | R / F | X |
| Jump / sprint | Space / Shift | A / click left stick |
| Crouch / prone | C or Ctrl / Z | tap B / hold B |
| Hold breath (scoped) | Shift | click left stick |
| Weapons | 1-9, wheel | Y next, LB previous |
| Lean left / right | hold Q / E | D-pad left / right (toggle) |
| Gear | G | RB |
| Inspect weapon | I | D-pad up |
| Change class / scoreboard (Iron Town) | L / hold Tab | D-pad down / hold Back |
| Chat / spectate | T / V | - |
| Pause / back | Esc | Start / B |
| Menus | mouse, or arrows + Enter | D-pad or stick + A, LB / RB for tabs |

Every keyboard and mouse binding can be changed in **SETTINGS > CONTROLS**:
pick an action, press the new key. A key already in use swaps places.

Stations are walk-up terminals: stand next to one, press **Use**, and a window
opens. Everything in it can be clicked or navigated with a pad.

## Cover and gear (Iron Town)

Lean around corners like the old tactical shooters: hold **Q** or **E**. Your
head and shoulders move out (never through a wall), the view tilts, and
everyone sees your robot lean - and can shoot what sticks out. Bots peek
corners too. You move at half speed while leaning and cannot sprint.

Gear, one kind per class:

| Gear | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| Frag grenade | Thrown explosive on a short fuse |
| Smoke grenade | A cloud that blocks sight: bots cannot see through it and name tags vanish behind it |
| Flashbang | Whites out the screen of anyone who sees it go off (worse looking straight at it); bots are stunned |
| Proximity mine | Thrown, arms after a second, goes off when an enemy comes close |
| Throwing knife | Fast and silent, deadly on a direct hit |
| Stim | Heals and puts out fire |
| Ammo pack | Refills reserve ammo for you and nearby teammates |

Perks: Lightweight, Plate Carrier, Fast Hands, Deadeye, Scavenger (kills refill
a magazine), Ghost (never on radar), Flak Jacket (less explosive damage),
Quick Draw (faster aiming and swaps) and Steady Hands (tighter hip fire).

The create-a-class screen shows each weapon's fire mode, calibre, damage,
rate of fire, magazine, reload time and stat bars with its fitted parts.
Fourteen weapons: pistol, revolver, SMG, PDW, shotgun, auto shotgun,
carbine, burst rifle, battle rifle, DMR, sniper, minigun, rocket launcher
and an energy lance.

## Weapons

Guns are built from parts. Each weapon is a frame (receiver, barrel, bore,
magazine shape, stock) plus numbers, and seven slots change both its stats
and its geometry: **optic, muzzle, barrel, underbarrel, magazine, stock and
ammunition**. Parts are limited by weapon class (no sniper scope on a
shotgun), and ammo depends on caliber: hollow point, armor piercing (punches
through thin walls), incendiary (burns), explosive rounds, slugs, dragon's
breath, flechettes, thermobaric and napalm rockets, overcharged cells.

Actions and reloads are animated per weapon: magazines drop and slap home,
shotguns load shell by shell (fire to interrupt), bolts and pumps are worked
after each shot, pistol slides kick, belt-fed guns tilt up to reload. Optics
with magnification (ACOG, sniper scope) switch to a scope overlay with sway
that crouching, going prone or holding your breath steadies. A suppressor
keeps you off the enemy radar; unsuppressed fire shows you on it.

## Performance

The engine is built for weak hardware:

* The world renders to a low-resolution target (window size / **PIXEL SIZE**)
  and is scaled up, so the GPU shades a fraction of the pixels. Raising pixel
  size in Settings is the biggest frame-rate lever; shadows can be turned off.
* Every model is baked into a few vertex-coloured meshes that share two
  materials, so hordes batch into a handful of instanced draws.
* Terrain is chunked, props are merged per region, particles are capped.
* The simulation uses a spatial grid and staggered AI. The headless stress
  test runs 1,500 enemies in about 2 ms per frame (dev profile).
* Network snapshots are 17 bytes per entity at 20 Hz (10 Hz in huge battles).
* Static collision (every wall, stair and prop) lives in a bucketed grid, and
  bots share a small per-frame pathfinding budget.
* While a menu shows the 3D showroom, the world camera is switched off.
